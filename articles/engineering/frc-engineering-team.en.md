I've known about FRC since my first year of high school. Now, in the summer after graduating, I've finally fulfilled my FRC dream.

I admit there were many things I didn't do well while leading this group of students from Changhua.

I only met them two days before the competition, so of course there were coordination problems. That's on me, and I have a lot to improve.

Looking back, deciding to take an FRC robot to an off-season competition after only two weeks of preparation was impulsive and honestly pretty reckless.

Thinking about it now, it was completely crazy.

We were still repairing the robot at our guesthouse just hours before the competition. Seriously, what were we doing?

At 1 a.m., we even ran to a hardware and household store to buy supplies, then rushed to catch the last MRT train.

We missed it.

Unbelievable.

I also met so many people at this competition: the captain from Banqiao Senior High School, Jay from Changhua Senior High School, and many others.

Thank you so much for helping us over those few days.

Without a bunch of teammates as crazy as us, there's no way the robot would have been fixed, let alone made it onto the field.

I learned a lot and talked with other teams, including the Yilan alliance and Ankang Senior High School, as well as people I haven't named here.

I haven't forgotten you. I remember every one of you.

After saying thank you, it's time to apologize.

There are many people I owe apologies to.

First, my own team.

I definitely need to improve how I assign tasks. Some responsibilities weren't distributed properly, and I didn't coordinate everything as well as I should have.

Those are things I need to work on.

And I really owe an apology to the neighbors at our guesthouse.

We were still fixing the robot at 3 a.m. to get ready for the competition.

With an actual electric drill.

It was really loud.

I should have managed that better. I'm sorry.

Looking back at those four days, an unbelievable amount happened.

Meeting the team just two days before the event. Deciding to enter an off-season FRC competition with only two weeks of preparation. Repairing the robot at the guesthouse hours before competing. Shopping for supplies at 1 a.m. Missing the last MRT train. Even drilling into the robot at 3 a.m.

It was completely insane.

But I'm truly grateful for everyone I met along the way.

From first hearing about FRC as a high school freshman to this summer after graduation:

**I finally fulfilled my FRC dream.**
