# From CTF to International Open-Source Collaboration: Why I Joined BugTraceAI

When I first got into cybersecurity, I thought CTF (Capture The Flag) competitions were pretty much the coolest, most impressive thing you could do.

But over the past year or two, after attending all kinds of cybersecurity events, I've learned that this field is about much more than just attacking and defending systems.

Especially now that the age of AI agents is arriving, I've started asking myself something:

**Do traditional CTF competitions still need to exist?**

I once came across an overseas CTF competition designed specifically for AI agents. It made me wonder: should we really limit AI agents to helping us develop software or maintain security?

What if they could take a deeper role in cybersecurity research? What might the future look like then?

Those questions were part of what led me to collaborate internationally with BugTraceAI.

## How Did I Find BugTraceAI?

I first noticed the team on social media.

Their CEO caught my attention because they had spoken in Hong Kong about cybersecurity and AI agents, and had been involved in related events.

Eventually, I had the chance to talk with the CEO.

I was upfront: I'm still a university student, so I can't make this project my only priority. Between school, competitions, and the pressure of research papers, I already have plenty pushing me forward.

To be honest, I didn't expect much at first.

I thought, why would a team this strong take in someone whose English isn't great and who doesn't attend some famous top university?

But then the CEO told me they were looking for developers with a cybersecurity background.

After putting myself forward, I got an opportunity I had really hoped for: helping identify bugs in BugTraceAI and finding areas that could be improved.

## Then I Met a Spanish Coding Wizard

Oh, right. I almost forgot to introduce my collaborator.

A coding wizard from Spain.

How good is he? When I heard he had graduated from Harvard, I honestly didn't know whether to feel excited or terrified.

Excited, because I actually had the chance to work with someone so capable.

Terrified, because I kept thinking:

**What if he thinks I'm terrible at this?**

But after talking through the project and collaborating a few times, I realized he's a genuinely great senior developer.

He's incredibly skilled, and there's so much I can learn from him.

I'll keep learning from him, and I hope to keep contributing to the development and maintenance of this team.

## Thank You to Everyone Who Gave Me a Chance

There are so many people I want to thank.

Thank you to the CEO for giving me this precious opportunity.

Thank you to the incredibly talented Harvard graduate. Without your contributions, I might never have had this chance to collaborate.

And thank you to everyone at BugTraceAI for welcoming me onto the team.

I'll keep working hard and contributing whatever I can. >_<

This is only a small part of what's been happening in my life lately.
